# How to Clean and Care for Your Wellness Devices

Proper cleaning is the single most important habit for extending the life of your personal massager — and protecting your health. Different materials require different care routines. Here is your complete maintenance guide.

## Why Cleaning Matters

Personal massagers come into contact with intimate areas of the body, making hygiene non-negotiable. Even if a device looks clean, microscopic bacteria and residue can accumulate on its surface. Non-porous materials (silicone, glass, metal) resist bacterial growth when cleaned properly. Porous materials (TPE, TPR) are much harder to sanitize — another reason we recommend body-safe materials only.

## Daily Cleaning Routine (After Every Use)

### Silicone Devices
1. Rinse with warm water to remove surface residue.
2. Apply a small amount of mild, fragrance-free soap or dedicated cleaner.
3. Lather for at least 20 seconds, covering the entire surface.
4. Rinse thoroughly with warm water.
5. Pat dry with a lint-free cloth or air dry on a clean towel.

**Important:** Avoid antibacterial soaps with harsh chemicals. They can degrade silicone over time. Use water-based lubricants only — silicone lubricants bond to silicone massagers and cause permanent damage.

### Glass Devices
Glass is the easiest material to clean. It is completely non-porous and can withstand:
- Warm water and mild soap (daily)
- Boiling water (3–5 minutes for deep sanitization)
- A 10% bleach solution (rinse thoroughly after)
- Top-rack dishwasher (only if confirmed safe by manufacturer)

Always let glass devices cool completely before use after sanitizing with hot water.

### Metal (Stainless Steel) Devices
Like glass, stainless steel is non-porous and highly resistant. Clean with warm water and mild soap, or boil for 3–5 minutes for deep sanitization. Avoid abrasive scrubbers — they can create micro-scratches where bacteria may accumulate.

## Deep Cleaning (Weekly or Between Partners)

For a deeper clean, use a dedicated device cleaner spray. These are formulated to sanitize without damaging materials. Alternatively, for non-motorized silicone, glass, or metal devices:
- Boil in water for 5–10 minutes
- For silicone devices with motors, wipe thoroughly with a 70% isopropyl alcohol solution, then rinse with water

## Storage Best Practices

Where and how you store your massager matters almost as much as how you clean it:

- **Separate storage:** Store each device individually. Different materials (especially silicone-on-silicone contact) can react and degrade when stored together.
- **Use pouches or cases:** A breathable fabric pouch or dedicated storage case protects against dust and lint. Avoid plastic bags, which trap moisture.
- **Cool, dry place:** Bathroom storage is convenient but exposes devices to humidity. A bedside drawer or closet shelf is better.
- **Remove batteries:** If your device uses disposable batteries, remove them between uses to prevent corrosion.

## When to Replace Your Device

Even with perfect care, devices have a lifespan. Here is when to say goodbye:
- Visible cracks, tears, or peeling on the surface
- Discoloration that does not wash off
- Persistent odor despite thorough cleaning
- Motor becoming significantly louder or weaker
- Silicone feeling sticky or tacky (signs of material breakdown)

With proper care, a high-quality silicone massager should last 2–5 years. Glass and metal devices can last a lifetime with proper handling.
